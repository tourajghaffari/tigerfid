# The decision brief for Story 2.1.7

The input to Omid's decision sitting (plan Story 2.1.7), drawn from the six
study documents so that the sitting is decisions only. It gathers every
question the sitting decides, with its options, the figures that bear on it,
its default and the strongest case against that default. **It decides
nothing.** Each default is the study's own choice: "A study chooses; the
sitting decides" (plan Sprint 2's D1, in the archive's § Executed sprints).

- **Its basis:** the studies at commit `cdaa8e5e`, after Story 2.1.16 landed
  and before its review did. A figure here is quoted from a study, never
  made here, except where a line is marked **this brief's own**.
- **How it cites:** `<document> § <section>`, with the requirement numbers
  [1]–[27] of `requirements.md`. The marks **INFERRED** and **ASSUMED** mean
  what the studies say they mean.
- **The sitting's output:** the rebuild's design spec at
  `docs/superpowers/specs/<date>-rebuild-design.md`, carrying
  `**Approved by:** Omid` and the date on one line. The close manifest's
  `rebuild-spec-approved` check reads that line (`docs/close/2.1.json`).

## The decisions

### 1. The FCC grant read

**The question:** which FCC ID a device carries, and whether the chosen PHY
is on its grant. The read is the sitting's, by Omid's hand in a browser,
"before the rebuild spec names an FCC ID or any part is bought"
(`radio-and-location.md` § The radio chosen; plan Story 2.1.7).

**What is read already:**
- TI's datasheet names FCC ID ZAT-1312PSIP-2, "15.247 Operation within the
  902-928 MHz band" (PSIP p54, p56);
- TI's certification note lists the modular approval letter and the five
  PHYs' test reports under ZAT-1312PSIP-3 (SWRA797 p14, p17);
- the FCC's search and two public mirrors refused a plain request (HTTP
  403), and Omid ruled "Write it as not read" (`parts-and-toolchains.md`
  § What was read, and what could not be).

**What the read settles:**
- **the PHY on -2:** the device carries "Contains FCC ID: ZAT-1312PSIP-2"
  (`radio-and-location.md` § Legality of the choice);
- **on -3 only:** it carries -3 (the same section);
- **on neither:** decision 2 loses its premise for every device beyond
  five. INFERRED, this brief's own: milestone 1's five devices still run
  under §15.23(a), but the route "Every transmitter is the CC1312PSIP, whose
  own modular grant covers it" (§ Legality of the choice) falls;
- **the grant's class,** frequency hopping or digital transmission 500 kHz
  wide: either holds, since the design hops ("It is harmless if the grant
  turns out to be the wide digital class", `radio-and-location.md`
  § Channels).

**The default:** read both grant pages first, before decision 2.
**The strongest case against reading it at the sitting:** it lengthens the
sitting for a read that buys nothing yet. Nothing is bought before the
bench story, so the spec could name no ID and carry the read as that
story's first gate (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § What the choice leaves to
later stories: "Before any part is bought").

### 2. The radio and location architecture

**The question:** option B, "one sub-GHz band, a certified module,
reader-assigned slots, and zones from Field Generators", on TI's CC1312PSIP
at its certified 300 kbit/s PHY, Wi-SUN's "Mode #5" (`radio-and-location.md`
§ The choice, § The radio chosen).

**The options** (`radio-and-location.md` § [3] against [2] first, its
table, and § The other options, one by one):

| Option | The study's verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A: a 433 MHz wake plus a sub-GHz reply, the original's split | declined | a wake field for [13]'s 5 s fails §15.231 in the US |
| **B: one sub-GHz band, 300 kbit/s, reader-assigned slots** | **chosen** | meets [3] and [2] together, on a certified module |
| B at 500 kbit/s or 1 Mbit/s | declined | −90 and −93 dBm on the module's sheet against −94 dBm at 300 kbit/s, and no certified module at those rates |
| B with several channels per Reader | declined for milestone 1 | the grant: "This transmitter must not be co-located or operating with any other antenna or transmitter" (PSIP p54) |
| C: dual-band, Bluetooth for setup | not chosen | no certified module found; it was left open with the seal's question, which Omid has since decided (§ Already decided) |
| D: Bluetooth Long Range | declined | under 976 replies a second even with perfect slots |
| E: LoRa | declined | at most 130 replies a second per channel |
| F: ultra-wideband | declined for milestone 1 | locates to the metre, which no requirement asks; −41.3 dBm/MHz average |

**The figures:**
- **[3]:** 1,363 slots a second on one channel (`radio-and-location.md`
  § [3] against [2] first). The conference's block leaves 1,296 a second,
  so a hall of 1,000 tags takes about 0.77 s, with 0.23 s left for retries
  (§ Anti-collision).
- **[12]:** the ward's 100 tags take 0.08 s (§ Anti-collision).
- **[21]:** with 12 Readers hopping on their own, another shares a Reader's
  channel in about 23 % of frames, which leaves about 1,050 slots a second
  against 200 (§ Channels).
- **[2]:** 20.7 dB of margin at the worst body loss, with the W5017's
  +0.90 dBi counted (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § The battery, the link at
  30 m); the radio study's own table gave 21.8 dB with +2 dBi ASSUMED
  (`radio-and-location.md` § Range). A crowd's other bodies and indoor
  fading, ASSUMED 10–20 dB, left "1.8–11.8 dB at the worst body loss", so
  [2] is "met on paper, not with confidence" (§ Range). INFERRED, this
  brief's own arithmetic: from 20.7 dB that is 0.7–10.7 dB.
- **[13]:** a move is shown in 3.0 s at a 1 s listen (`radio-and-location.md`
  § Zones; `parts-and-toolchains.md` § Each worn tag's year).
- **The law:** milestone 1's five devices need no authorisation of their own
  (§15.23(a)); beyond five, the module's grant covers every transmitter
  (§15.212), at a certified PHY with the SDK's default settings
  (`radio-and-location.md` § Legality of the choice).

**The default:** option B on the CC1312PSIP ("Story 2.1.7 decides, with this
document's choice as its default", `radio-and-location.md` § What the
choice leaves to later stories).

**The strongest case against:** the whole design rests on one vendor's
module.
- Its certification holds only with "The default settings in the SDK"
  (SWRA797 p6, in `radio-and-location.md` § The radio chosen).
- The SDK's licence is for "use only with TI Devices", and a published
  firmware image would carry TI's object code. The firmware initiative takes
  that to the ASK-OMID register before any image is published
  (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § The licences of the reference designs read).
- SysConfig's and TI Arm Clang's licences are unread (`parts-and-toolchains.md`
  § The toolchains); the SDK also names the GCC route.
- The study read no certified module from another vendor.
- The module is a 0.5 mm-pitch package that is not hand-soldered
  (`radio-and-location.md` § The radio chosen), which puts every build route
  on paste or an assembly service (decision 8).

INFERRED, this brief's own reading: the anti-goals rule out "Dependence on a
paid tool or a single vendor's cloud". Every tool here is free, and TI's
cloud tools are declined (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § The toolchains), so
the case against is a single vendor's part, not its cloud.

### 3. Story 2.1.16's two answers, to confirm or overrule

The sitting had not run when Story 2.1.16 closed the review's Minors A and
C, "so the study writes its own answers to Minors A and C, for the sitting
to confirm or overrule" (plan Story 2.1.16's Outcome). Neither binds
milestone 1's build, which has one Reader in the US. Both bind the design
the spec records for the scenarios.

**3a. The conference hall's second Reader** ([2]'s "a second reader";
`radio-and-location.md` § Zones, The conference hall's second Reader).
- **The default: it listens on the first Reader's network.** It sends no
  beacon and has no block, and the host keeps one copy of each reply. The
  hall stays one block, and [9] holds by 22 % in an area of the hall (122.5
  mAh a year, 1.22 years; `parts-and-toolchains.md` § Each worn tag's year).
- **The other way: its own network.** With no Field Generator added, [9]
  holds by 9 % beside the edge (137.4 mAh, 1.09 years; 0.86 at the floor). A
  badge that rejoins at each crossing adds up to 61.7 mAh a year at ten
  crossings a day (ASSUMED): 184.2 mAh, 0.81 years, and [9] unmet.
- **The strongest case against the default:** its acknowledgement path is
  unmeasured. A reply that only the second Reader hears is acknowledged in
  the first Reader's next beacon once the host passes it on, or the tag
  retries. If every check-in were retried once, a badge in the hall would
  need 126.4 mAh, 1.19 years. The build measures the path.

**3b. The EU's channels** (`radio-and-location.md` § Channels).
- **The default: each Reader keeps one channel, and Readers beside each
  other take different ones,** four of the band's five in a repeating 2 × 2
  pattern.
- **The other way: one channel for every Reader,** declined. The edge bay's
  two Field Generators would meet 0.32 % of the time: at worst 40 s, once in
  about 3.5 hours, without bound as the crystals agree.
- **The strongest case against the default:** a tag that has lost its
  timing searches the channels one at a time and finds its Reader within
  four searches, about 15 minutes. The pattern takes four of the five
  channels a warehouse has.

### 4. One core or three, with the sealTag inside it

**The question:** do the seal, the fob and the card share one electronics
core in three enclosures, or take three boards (spec §3.7)? The sitting
also decides milestone 1's sealTag design here (plan Story 2.1.7).

**The sealTag is a new design either way.** The 2012 sealTag is an ISO 15693
logging seal with no active radio, so it cannot send [14]'s alarm
(`original-system.md` § What this means for the study). The new one keeps
its form, a band or seal whose loop is wired to a pin, and replaces its
electronics. Its tamper alarm reaches the host in about 1.5 s against [14]'s
5 s (INFERRED, `parts-and-toolchains.md` § One core or three).

**For one core** (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § One core or three, each
INFERRED there): one radio layout under the certification's layout
conditions (SWRA797 §3.1.3, §3.1.4, §3.2.2); one firmware image, with the
tag style as a setting; one board in larger quantity; one set of
certification conditions; one board for a beginner to learn and debug.

**The cost of three boards:** 23.25 more hand-soldered, 109.67 more partly
assembled and 117.12 more fully assembled (`tiered-cost.md` § How the build
is counted).

**The default: one core,** sized for the fob, with the sensor, the tamper
loop and the badge's buttons as fitting options. It goes into the seal, the
fob and the card. The warehouse item tag is a later variant of the same
schematic with a CR2450 holder (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § One core or
three; the totals in `tiered-cost.md` are priced on it).

**The strongest case against:** the card gives up range. A ground plane
shorter than a quarter wave, 8.2 cm at 915 MHz, loses efficiency and
bandwidth (SWRA797 p9). A card is wide enough to approach it, and a core
sized for the fob is not. Every board also pays for every option's
footprints. The build's range test ([2]) measures the card's cost. INFERRED,
this brief's own: [2]'s margin in decision 2 already assumes a small ground
plane (the tag antenna's −5 dBi is +1.0 dBi "less 6 dB ASSUMED for a small
ground plane and the body", `parts-and-toolchains.md` § The battery), so
three boards would add range to the card rather than one core taking it from
the paper margin.

### 5. The warehouse item tag's cell

The item tag is not a milestone 1 device. This decision binds the core's
warehouse variant.

**The figures** (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § [27], the 12 s row; every bay,
a Reader's edge included):

| Cell, at a 12 s listen | Plain item tag | Cold chain |
|---|---|---|
| CR2032 (150 mAh usable) | 124.2 mAh: 1.21 of five years | 136.9 mAh: 1.10 |
| CR2450 (396 mAh usable) | 143.5 mAh: 2.76 times the need | 156.2 mAh: 2.54 |

- The CR2450 is 4.5 mm wider and 1.8 mm thicker (§ [27]), and costs no
  more: 0.95 a tag with its holder against the CR2032's 1.38 (`tiered-cost.md`
  § Both cells, where the sitting chooses).
- [26]'s 30 s bounds the listen at 14.5 s, and 12 s gives 25 s
  (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § [27]).
- A warehouse that takes no second Field Generators at its Readers' edges
  keeps 1.16 and 1.05 of five years with a second wake every fifth listen
  (`radio-and-location.md` § Zones, A Reader's edge).

**The default:** the slower wake, a listen every 12 s, which keeps five
years on a CR2032 on paper; and, on top of it, the CR2450 wherever the item
takes the larger tag, the cold-chain tags first (`parts-and-toolchains.md`
§ [27]).

**The strongest case against the CR2450:** the larger holder makes the item
tag a variant of the core's schematic, and puts a larger tag on every item.
**Against a CR2032 alone:** the cold-chain tag's 10 % margin at 100,000 tags,
where [27]'s own reason is the battery-change workload, about 274 changes a
day at a 1-year battery and 55 at five (`requirements.md` [27]).

**The cells' maker, for decisions 5 and 6.** "The cells the build buys are
the sitting's to name" (`tiered-cost.md` § Both cells, where the sitting
chooses). The battery figures rest on Energizer's sheets: 235 mAh for a
CR2032 and 620 mAh for a CR2450, each to 2.0 V. The usable 150 mAh is still
ASSUMED, with a floor of 117.5 mAh (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § The
battery). The priced cells are Maxell's CR2032 (220 mAh) and a CR2450 whose
page names no maker (`tiered-cost.md` § Both cells). No study recommends a
maker. If the sitting names none, the bench story's first battery
measurements decide (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § What the choice leaves to
later stories).

### 6. The nursery tag's cell, if the measurement falls short

**The figures** (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § Each worn tag's year):
- a tag in a room, at a 1 s listen: 120.7 mAh a year, 1.24 years, so [18]
  holds by 24 %; 0.97 years at the 117.5 mAh floor;
- a tag that never leaves the corridor: 251.0 mAh, 0.60 years. [18] is
  unmet on paper for a tag in the corridor more than about 5.4 hours a day.

**The routes, each with its cost** (the same section): measure first, on the
LP-XDS110ET; a CR2450, 1.58 years for a tag that never leaves the corridor
and 3.01 for one there 2 hours a day; shorter stretches, 2.3 µA saved for
each neighbour fewer, at the cost of more Field Generators; one conversion
per reading (decision 7); and the fallback in which the Field Generators
listen and the tags only transmit, which the radio study has not designed.

**The default: measure first, then the CR2450 for any tag the measurement
leaves short.** INFERRED there: those are the adults' fobs, which take the
larger cell. A baby's tag spends its stay in the rooms and holds on its
CR2032 on paper.

**The strongest case against:** at the floor even a room's tag falls short,
0.97 years, and a baby's band "may not" take a CR2450. If the measurement
lands near the floor, a baby's tag is left with routes that cost accuracy or
Field Generators, not a cell. The measurement comes after the sitting, so
the spec records a rule, not a cell.

The conference badge has the same shape: 0.96 years at the floor in an area
of the hall, and its route is a 3 s listen there, 1.71 years with [8] in
about 7 s (the same section).

### 7. The temperature sensor's accuracy, and its setting

**The question:** does the spec name an accuracy for the TMP117 on the
nursery's and the cold-chain tags, and does a reading take eight averages
or one conversion? "The requirements set no accuracy for the sensor", so
any figure the study picked "would be a new requirement (plan D1)"
(`parts-and-toolchains.md` § The tags).

**The figures:**
- the TMP117 reads ±0.1 °C from −20 to 50 °C, specified at "8 averages,
  1-Hz conversion cycle" (TMP117 p6). A reading at eight averages costs
  7.3 mAh a year at one every 20 s (`parts-and-toolchains.md` § Each worn
  tag's year);
- one conversion repeats to about ±3 LSB, ±0.023 °C, against ±1 LSB at
  eight (TMP117 p6, p12). It saves 6.4 mAh a year: a nursery room's tag
  needs 114.3 mAh, 1.31 years (the same section), and the cold-chain item
  tag 131.6 mAh, 1.14 of five years (§ [27]);
- the module's own sensor reads ±3.5 °C at 2 °C resolution (PSIP p32), too
  coarse for a cold-chain limit (INFERRED, § The tags).

**The default:** no accuracy named, and eight averages, as every battery
figure is priced.

**The strongest case against:** margin. One conversion lifts the room tag
from 24 % to 31 % and the cold-chain tag from 10 % to 14 % (this brief's own
arithmetic, from 1.31 and 1.14), and its repeatability suits "a limit
someone set on screen" (`requirements.md` § Ground rules). Its cost is an
accuracy the sensor is not specified at. Whatever is named, the guides never
call the tag a medical device or a monitor of vital signs (spec C8).

rebuild's builders are beginners. INFERRED there: the guides say so plainly,
and the debugger stays on the bench. A debugger outside TI's terms was not
searched.

### 9. What the spec says of the EU build

**The question:** the US build is priced and the EU build is named, not
priced (`tiered-cost.md` § The EU build, plan Sprint 3's D8). Does the spec
carry the EU build that way, or keep milestone 1 and its guides to the US?

**The figures:**
- the EU has no home-built exemption and no module grant. The route is a
  radio whose maker names EN 300 220, operation inside band 47 (25 mW e.r.p.,
  a duty cycle under 1 %), and the builder's own care for the Directive's
  Article 3. "The decision sitting should know that the EU build rests on
  the builder's compliance" (`radio-and-location.md` § Legality of the
  choice);
- the EU parts that differ: the tag's 868 MHz chip antenna, unnamed; the
  W5017 at 868 MHz, not read; the 230 V plugs; and the Reader's 25 mW
  e.r.p., a setting (`tiered-cost.md` § The EU build).

**The default:** the EU build named, not priced, with its legality stated
as the builder's own compliance.

**The strongest case against:** the anti-goal "A transmitter a beginner
cannot legally run". In the EU the beginner carries the compliance
themselves, and the study "cannot make that route as firm as the US grant"
(the same section).
